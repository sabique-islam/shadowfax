# Tells

Flag **clusters**, not a single word. Isolated "crucial" or one em dash is not proof.

## Lexicon (swap or cut)

Tier 1 (dead giveaways, still drop if they sit alone): delve, tapestry, vibrant, seamless, robust (as puff), pivotal, testament, leverage, underscore, landscape (figurative), nestled, realm, foster, empower, unlock, groundbreaking, multifaceted, synergy, crucial, showcase, harness, unpack, meticulous, transformative, myriad, cornerstone, catalyst, embark, garner, interplay, intricate, elevate, utilize, facilitate

## Copula dodge

"serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "features" when "is" or "has" is true.

## Structure

- "It's not X, it's Y" / "not only X but Y"
- Forced groups of three
- Negative listing ("Not a tool. Not a framework. A philosophy.")
- Throat-clearing: "Here's the thing", "It's worth noting", "Let's dive in", "At its core", "Here's what you need to know"
- Demonstrative kicker: "That instinct backfires.", "This is the whole point."
- Importance-flagging: "This matters.", "Make no mistake."
- False agency: "the decision emerges", "the data tells us"
- Wh-cleft opener: "What makes this hard is..."
- Generic closer: "The future looks bright.", "Exciting times ahead."
- Present-participle tails that add no fact: "highlighting...", "ensuring...", "showcasing..."
- Vague attribution: "Experts believe", "Industry reports suggest"

## Chatbot residue

"Great question!", "I hope this helps", "Let me know if you'd like", "Certainly!", "Of course!", "Would you like me to", "Let me think"

## Filler

"In order to" → "To". "Due to the fact that" → "Because". "It is important to note that" → delete. "Additionally / Furthermore / Moreover" stacked at paragraph starts. "First and foremost." "At the end of the day."

## Rhythm

Three sentences the same length in a row. Every paragraph ending punchy. Stacked short fragments for drama.

## Formatting (platform)

Emails and DMs: no markdown headers, no bold-label bullets. Social: no hashtag stacks, no emoji bullets. Headings: sentence case. No em dashes unless the founder's sample uses them. Straight quotes in paste-ready drafts.

## Keep (voice, not slop)

Earned short beat after a long sentence. A contrast the author would defend. First-person when they own the claim. Specific ugly detail. Mixed feelings. Contractions.
