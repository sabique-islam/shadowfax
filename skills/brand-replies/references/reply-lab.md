# Brand reply lab

## What transfers from Zomato/Swiggy-style work

Shared everyday context, short native language, a recognizable voice, and a brand willing to be the butt of the joke are useful ingredients. They are hypotheses about craft, not evidence that every brand needs a cheeky persona. Do not imitate a delivery app's voice for a serious product by default.

The verified [Swiggy Voice of Hunger agency case](https://www.dentsu.com/il/en/our-work/dentsu-webchutney-swiggy-voice-of-hunger) used a playful platform interaction, creator seeding, and real-time replies. Its reported 150,000 voice notes concern a produced campaign with resources, not proof that random witty comments convert. [Zomato's 2019 brand guide](https://www.zomato.com/content_guidelines.pdf) is visual guidance; it is not a sourced collection of successful comments. Verify original posts when the founder wants a real teardown.

## Capture the opportunity before the line

Record original URL, timestamp/timezone, author, post text or supplied excerpt, relevant replies, community rules, audience overlap, and why this brand belongs. Read enough of the thread to distinguish friendly banter from a complaint or sensitive disclosure. A large account is not automatically a useful destination.

If the original is unavailable, ask for its text and continue only on the supplied context, labeled freshness unverified. If the moment passed, a timeless own-channel observation may be a different task; do not present it as a live reply opportunity.

## Find a voice with examples

Read three real samples if available. Identify language, rhythm, how direct the account is, and who gets teased. Start with the founder's demonstrated register. Hinglish is a choice rooted in audience language, not an instruction to add “yaar” to English copy. Code-switch where the thought naturally changes; don't explain the joke twice.

A short voice card can say: conversational English/Hinglish, one sentence, self-own allowed, no jokes at customers' expense, no link unless asked. Label it proposed if the account has no voice history. It does not become permanent brand truth without founder agreement.

## Original worked examples

All situations and lines below are fictional teaching examples. They are not attributed Zomato/Swiggy posts, current trends, customer claims, or ready-to-post opportunities. Replace them with the actual source context before drafting for a founder.

| Supplied situation | Original candidate | Mechanism / use condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A food lover says choosing dinner took longer than cooking | “Dinner had a selection committee.” | Reframes a mundane decision; no sales link needed |
| Friends say they will split one dessert | “The spoon-to-friendship ratio looks risky.” | Tiny social tension without attacking anyone |
| A team jokes that its meeting needs another meeting | “This meeting has a sequel before a conclusion.” | Literal continuation; fits workplace voice |
| A developer shares a harmless CSV full of nested commas | “The commas have formed a committee.” | One specific artifact detail; no claim about fixing it |
| A founder jokes about too many dashboard tabs | “Your browser has started a second shift.” | Shared workflow frustration |
| Someone says “bas ek bite” beside a giant dessert | “Ek bite ka scope thoda badh gaya.” | Natural Hinglish exaggeration; fits the post's language |
| A group cannot pick what to order | “Dinner se pehle coalition banegi.” | Group-decision joke; avoid if thread context is politically charged |
| A person names three snack breaks as their schedule | “Calendar mein snacks ke beech kaafi meetings hain.” | Reverses priority without a product pitch |
| A maker admits renaming final files repeatedly | “final_final has requested a promotion.” | Personifies a specific habit |
| A customer celebrates a milestone | “That deserves a screenshot before the next tab opens.” | Celebrate first; don't hijack credit |
| Someone describes a serious billing problem | No joke. Acknowledge the problem and offer the actual support route. | Service takes precedence; no invented refund promise |
| A competitor has an outage affecting users | Offer useful, factual help only if relevant and permitted. | A roast exploits someone else's bad day and may overclaim reliability |

These lines are starting points for craft study, not a reusable comment batch. If the same line fits 20 unrelated posts, make it more specific or reject it.

## Draft and reject

Three candidates can explore different mechanisms: literal interpretation, self-own, and callback. Select on context fit, voice fit, readability, and whether the intended person is likely to welcome it. Numeric humor scores create false precision.

Reject: a sales CTA bolted to a punchline; “we're savage” boasting; generic laughing emoji; a joke needing three lines of explanation; copying a competitor; teasing a customer reporting harm; a callback to something that never happened. A reply can fail even when it passes the profanity check.

## Measurement without turning into a bot

For a small human-run pilot, specify a handful of relevant opportunities over a week, why each was selected, and time spent. Watch welcomed conversation, relevant profile visits where available, source-mentioned inbound, and subsequent first value. Do not mass-comment, use fake accounts, or infer causality from likes. No brand mentions in a customer conversation can be the right outcome.

For testing two styles, match topic/audience/time as well as possible and change one feature. Posting identical lines under many people is spam, not an experiment. If people object, stop and reassess voice/context; do not argue that they failed to understand the joke.
