# A deal is a set of known commitments

Distinguish the user with a problem, a champion willing to help, the person controlling budget, and any security/procurement gate. One person can fill several roles; a title alone establishes none of them. Keep unknowns explicit rather than forcing every conversation into a framework score.

## Ask for the missing fact

| Unknown | Useful discovery direction | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The actual job | Last time they tried it, current workaround, frequency | A list of leading “would you use” questions |
| Consequence | Their observed delay, manual effort, risk, or missed outcome | Invented dollars lost or a generic ROI multiple |
| Decision | Who evaluates, who approves, and what evidence they need | Assuming the most senior person signs |
| Timing | Their real trigger, current commitments, and desired next step | A fake expiring discount |
| Ability to deliver | A bounded test using their relevant example | A feature tour or unsupported roadmap promise |

A useful demo reproduces the buyer's job, shows the result, and names limits. Use a synthetic or consented redacted example when inputs are private. Define what the pilot will prove, how long it runs, who participates, what it costs, and what happens to the data/work afterward. A free trial is not a signed deal.

## Objections are information

Answer the stated concern first. A price objection may be a real budget constraint; a timing objection may be a real contract. Do not assume a hidden psychological objection. If the answer is unknown, say what needs checking. If it is a nonfit, end the pursuit plainly. A clear no does not need another closing question.

For price requests, give the actual price/metric and known conditions. For a champion, prepare a forwardable decision brief with their job, required evidence, total cost, material limitations, and next action. For a complex account, coordinate an introduction instead of contacting every person cold.

## Offer brief

What useful result, for whom, what inputs and effort, exact scope/exclusions, price or pilot cost, actual terms, proof/acceptance criterion, and the next action. Match the commitment to their request: an explanation can be enough for curiosity; an actionable trial may suit active evaluation. There is no universal rule that every first contact must hide the product or that every interested user needs a call.

Close with the next step they actually agreed to and a real date if one exists. Never book without authorization or claim calendar availability not checked. Put actual observations in run-state and keep planned follow-ups separate from completed activity.
