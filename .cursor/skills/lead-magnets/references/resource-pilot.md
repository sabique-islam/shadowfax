# A useful resource with a credible next job

## Choose the artifact from the decision it helps

| Resource | User's useful result | Possible paid next job | Trap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Checklist | Complete a task without missing known steps | Run or continuously check the workflow | Generic advice that needs no product |
| Template | Produce one finished deliverable | Repeat, collaborate, or manage it at scale | Collectors who never use the artifact |
| Worked example | Understand a difficult job through a reproducible case | Apply it to their own real input | Invented results or unsupported benchmarks |
| Small dataset | Make a specific comparison or choice | Freshness, coverage, or workflow integration | Unclear rights and ongoing maintenance |
| Decision guide | Choose among honest alternatives | Trial the relevant option | A disguised sales brochure |

A resource must work independently of a sale. The paid bridge is a useful continuation, not an arbitrary call-to-action. Current alternative comparisons need inspected sources and dates. An unverified savings estimate is not proof.

## Example: a complete first result

Hypothetical importer: a two-page preflight checklist with a synthetic three-row CSV, a column-mapping example, common failure cases, and a way to check the result. Paid next job: safely repeat the import for a larger recurring file. Build the sample and verify the instructions before writing a post promising the checklist.

Hypothetical meal-planning product: a one-week plan using a small ingredient set, with substitutions and clear assumptions about portions. Paid next job: adapt future plans to a household's actual constraints. Do not invent nutrition/medical claims or savings figures. If those become necessary, use current appropriate sources and the relevant domain expertise.

## Decide how access works

A public link minimizes fulfillment effort and lets people evaluate the artifact. Optional save/update capture can add value when the reader wants it. A requested-delivery mechanism can make sense where the audience already asks for resources, but creates an operational promise and may exclude people who prefer not to comment publicly.

Do not choose comment keywords merely to inflate engagement. Do not count a like as a request. A request for the artifact authorizes that bounded fulfillment in the stated context; further sales contact needs its own relationship/context, not an assumption that all requesters entered a campaign.

## Original teaching copy

These are hypothetical specimens, not live drafts or verified product claims.

Invitation for an existing relevant community thread: “I made a CSV preflight checklist with a three-row sample and a mapping example. It covers duplicate headers and inconsistent rows. The link is here if it helps with this import.” Use a real working link only where permitted; do not claim the checklist fixes an untested case.

Requested delivery message: “Here's the checklist you asked for: [actual resource link]. The sample is synthetic. Start with the header check on page one.” This fulfills a request without adding a meeting ask or treating the person as a qualified buyer. In a finished draft, resolve the link and verify the page reference.

A request for feedback can follow when welcomed: ask which step they used or what remained unclear. Do not pretend that downloading the guide means they used it, and do not send repeated messages to people who gave no indication they wanted them.

## Fulfillment record

Requester ID matched to the original request; request URL/reference and date; promised artifact/version; preferred or stated delivery surface; owner; due date if actually promised; delivered date/reference when reported; duplicate/decline/failed-delivery state; follow-up boundary. Store personal records locally under the ignored outreach data directory. A delivery draft is not a completed delivery event.

If capacity is limited, change the invitation before accepting more requests. If the artifact link fails, fix access and provide the actual corrected route rather than issuing a fictional apology timeline. A gift or extra reward is not compensation for an unfinished useful resource.

## Pilot and readout

Proposed learning pilot: ten relevant people over a week, with a fixed founder-hours cap. Choose success thresholds from the intended decision, such as actual use by several requesters and two qualified requests for the paid next job. Those are proposed decision rules, not benchmarks.

Track unique relevant exposure where knowable, explicit requests, fulfilled requests, observed/reported use, qualified next action, and later product first value. Name missing denominators instead of guessing conversion rates. Distinguish ten downloads from ten users and ten users from ten buyers.

Many requests but no use: access, quality, or a curiosity-only audience. Use without a paid next job: the resource may be useful but poorly connected to the product. First-value attempts that fail: activation. No relevant requests: investigate the job or placement before producing another guide. Change one variable per next test.
