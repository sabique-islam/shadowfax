# Referral and affiliate operating playbook

## First determine which relationship exists

A customer referral transfers an earned recommendation. An affiliate distributes an offer for compensation. An ambassador may contribute expertise or community work with noncash recognition. They need different asks and economics; do not label paid distribution organic word of mouth.

Inspect real repeat usage and recommendations before adding rewards. If those are absent, ask a few satisfied users whether they would introduce one peer and why. A no-reward introduction pilot tests social fit without creating incentive liability. It is not a retention study.

## Program specification

| Decision | Specify before a pilot |
| --- | --- |
| Eligibility | Which existing users, territories, and friend/account types qualify |
| Trigger | Observed value moment when recommendation feels deserved |
| Qualification | Friend's verified first value or nonrefunded purchase, not just a click |
| Reward | Who receives what; delivery cost, limits, and any expiry |
| Attribution | First/last eligible referral, window, cross-device policy, existing-account rule |
| Settlement | Hold period, refund reversal, self-referral rule, disputes and owner |
| Experience | Pending/qualified/paid/ineligible states and clear explanations |
| Budget | Maximum reward liability and automatic pause condition for the human owner |

For affiliate agreements also specify eligible net receipts, commission period, renewal treatment, coupon overlap, reporting, disclosure, use of brand assets, termination, and post-termination commissions. Do not casually promise lifetime commission. Before execution, verify applicable advertising/disclosure rules in current authoritative sources.

## Economics: use the event you actually bought

Total program cost = fulfilled rewards at incremental cost + commissions + discounts reducing receipts + administration/support + fraud/refund loss. Do not double-count a discount already removed from net receipts.

Attributed CAC = program cost / attributed qualified customers. Incremental CAC needs an estimate of customers who would not otherwise arrive. A holdout can help; a referral link alone cannot provide that counterfactual. Do not subtract organic purchases by guess.

Hypothetical worksheet: 100 eligible users, 20 recommend, 30 unique friends visit, 10 reach first value, 4 pay and survive the refund window. If rewards cost $40 in total and support costs $20, attributed CAC is $15. If one of the four would have purchased anyway, a defensible incremental estimate would be $20; without evidence that remains a sensitivity calculation, not a measured number.

For product credits, use both expected marginal cost and the possible loss of upgrades. A $10 face-value reward need not cost $10, but it is not free merely because no cash changes hands. Forecast worst-case eligible payouts before setting a cap.

## A manageable first test

Choose a cohort with evidence of value, propose at most one incentive, and define a comparison before sending. Track unique eligible users → prompted → recommenders → unique referred people → first value → retained/paying customers → settled rewards. Separate referrers from referrals in every denominator.

A small cohort tells you where the flow breaks. It rarely identifies a precise lift. Report raw counts, cohort dates, and uncertainty; do not import universal viral coefficients or LTV uplifts.

## Failure diagnosis

No recommendations: weak trust, timing, or social risk; a larger prize may hide the problem. Many referrals but low activation: targeting or landing mismatch. Activation but poor retention: reward-seeking or product mismatch. High sales but no margin: economics. Codes appearing on coupon sites: attribution capture rather than new demand; revisit terms before scaling.

## Example mechanics, not templates to copy blindly

[Dropbox's program documentation](https://help.dropbox.com/storage-space/earn-space-referring-friends) ties storage rewards to qualifying account steps and caps. This verifies a product-native reward design, not its causal growth effect. [Harry's founder account](https://tim.blog/2014/07/21/harrys-prelaunchr-email/) concerns a prelaunch email campaign; it does not establish paid-customer economics for a SaaS referral program.

A hypothetical importer invitation can say: “If someone on your team is still cleaning these exports by hand, here's the preflight we used. They can try it on a sample first.” Add a reward disclosure only if an actual program exists. Never imply the founder already helped this recipient unless the history supports it.
