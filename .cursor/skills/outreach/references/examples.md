# Outreach that earns a relevant reply

Examples below are hypothetical teaching situations. Names, events, offers, and results must be replaced with verified prospect facts and actual product capabilities before use. They are not source evidence or live drafts.

## Select the moment

| Verified signal | Useful offer | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A filed issue with a reproducible failure | Test a minimal redacted input and report compatibility | Claiming the issue is solved before testing |
| A requested workaround in a community | A concise working answer in the permitted format | Treating a help thread as an ad slot |
| A stated switching decision | A migration map or reversible trial | Invented urgency around a competitor |
| An expert's specific lesson with a visible gap | An example that helps their audience finish the next step | Flattery unrelated to their actual work |
| A relevant past user stopped recurring work | Ask whether that job still exists | Pretending to be personally worried about them |

Use evidence of the recipient's job, not trivia about their school or dog. A funding announcement does not by itself prove they need the product. Record source URL/date and what remains uncertain.

## Artifact-first cold email

Hypothetical context: Maya's issue #412 shows an importer failing on a 50k-row file. The founder has actually tested a relevant streaming approach, but not Maya's file.

Subject: the import in #412

Maya, #412 stops at 50k rows before the preview. We process the file in chunks, though I haven't tested your export. If you can share a synthetic header plus two representative rows, I can check whether our preview handles that format.

Why it works: one observed fact, a capability the founder can substantiate, an explicit unknown, and a small job-specific ask. Do not request production data as the default.

## Useful public answer

Hypothetical context: a thread shows duplicate CSV headers.

Those two columns have the same header, so a name-based map can overwrite one. Rename them before the import and check the preview against a sample row. I work on an importer; happy to explain the mapping if that helps.

The answer stands on its own. A product link belongs only if it is useful and permitted. Never post the same answer across unrelated threads.

## Switching offer

Hypothetical context: a prospect explicitly says exports are blocking a migration, and the founder can support the required fields.

Your export thread mentions attachments and custom fields as the blockers. I can map one synthetic project into our trial and show which fields transfer and which don't. Want the mapping first?

Compatibility still needs verification. Prepare the transfer map, unsupported fields, cost, and rollback before implying migration is easy. Do not exploit a temporary outage with an unsupported reliability boast.

## Follow-up is new information

A follow-up should answer an unresolved question, provide a result the founder actually produced, or close the loop. Time elapsed is not new value.

Hypothetical: “I tested the synthetic header you posted. Both duplicate columns survive the preview if they have unique names; attachments still need a separate step. Here's the two-row sample. Useful, or is the import no longer a priority?” Use only if the test occurred and the recipient has not declined.

Without a reply, one relevant follow-up can be enough for a small pilot. The founder chooses timing based on the relationship; there is no universal magic interval. Stop on refusal, opt-out, clear nonfit, or the predefined no-response limit. Never continue on another channel to evade a refusal.

## Rejected opener

“I came across your company and loved what you're building. Would love to hop on a quick call to explore synergies.” No observed job, no reason it is this person, and an expensive ask. Passing a banned-word linter alone would not make it useful.

## A bounded first test

Use five to ten named, relevant prospects as a proposed learning pilot. Keep one segment, one offer, and one channel. Track delivered messages, job-specific replies, qualified conversations, accepted pilots, first value, and later payment. Do not optimize open rates or combine delivery failures with recipient rejection. Report counts and exact objections; tiny samples do not prove a conversion benchmark.

Before scaling, diagnose: no relevant reply → signal/offer; interest but no next action → ask/friction; pilots fail → product/capability; useful trials never pay → buyer/value. The next move need not be more outreach.
