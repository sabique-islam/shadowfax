# Partnership pilot playbook

## Choose a partnership that creates a specific result

| Model | Shared deliverable | Why partner participates | Pilot measure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Niche creator | Real workflow demonstration | Useful episode and fair compensation/access | Qualified first-value users |
| Newsletter | One problem-specific placement | Valuable reader resource and agreed fee/swap | Qualified conversations or activation |
| Complementary tool | Joint template or recipe | Makes its users more successful | Activated users completing both steps |
| Specialist agency | Client workflow improvement | Faster delivery or defined referral terms | Qualified introductions and completed pilots |
| Expert workshop | Live teardown and take-home artifact | Serves the same audience | Attendees using the artifact afterward |

A logo swap without distribution or a user benefit is not a collaboration brief. A marketplace listing is a search surface; verify buyers use it and who owns distribution.

## Research the candidate

Read three relevant recent pieces if available, their audience's questions, and one previous collaboration. Capture URLs/dates. Look for evidence of the exact job, geography/language, and buying role. Paid reach, subscribers, and engagement supplied by the partner are self-reported until verified. Never infer private performance from follower count.

Prioritize audience overlap, trust on this subject, an unmet audience need, willingness to demonstrate honestly, and operational fit. A rejection reason matters more than a decorative 92/100 score. Keep a record of who was rejected and why.

## Concrete hypothetical collaboration

An importer founder finds a data-cleaning educator whose audience keeps asking about broken CSV exports. Offer a 20-minute teardown using a synthetic file, a downloadable validation checklist, and a trial link that opens the same sample. The educator may criticize limitations. The founder supplies working assets and answers; the educator supplies distribution and teaching.

Draft structure: one sentence about the specific lesson; one audience question it leaves open; the joint deliverable; the founder's contribution; an easy “worth outlining?” ask. Avoid “love your content,” vague mutual benefit, and an unsolicited deck. If no fee is known, ask for the relevant rate rather than inventing one.

## Pilot agreement

Write the deliverable, channel/account, release window, approver, edit/revision limit, asset rights and reuse period, compensation (cash/product/service), cancellation or missed-deadline handling, measurement access, and a named owner. Keep truthful criticism possible. Do not promise a positive review or borrow a creator's likeness without agreement.

For a newsletter, agree placement location, actual sends, audience segment, tracking method, report timing, and a remedy for non-delivery. Opens can be distorted; a verified delivered audience and resulting actions matter more.

## Break-even and attribution

Hypothetical: $400 fee + $100 production cost = $500 pilot. If first-period contribution per new customer is $100, five incremental customers break even before overhead. Do not divide by gross contract value. With no conversion history, show scenarios instead of a forecast. Include founder hours and the cost of servicing the offer.

Use partner-specific landing parameters, an optional code, and a self-reported source question. Deduplicate across these signals; do not count one customer three times. Define window, new/existing user eligibility, refunds, and overlap with other campaigns. A small staggered pilot or holdout may inform incrementality; last-click credit alone does not.

## Follow-through

A successful pilot earns another collaboration when the audience was right, the deliverable worked, and contribution supports it. Large clicks with poor first value suggest an offer/activation mismatch. No delivery means an operational failure, not a failed channel. Do not sign a long exclusivity term before learning whether the partner can reach buyers.

No invented partner case results are needed to justify a pilot. The source fit and a bounded useful collaboration are enough to propose a test, explicitly as an assumption.

## Consumer example: an ingredient-first creator

Hypothetical: a meal-planning app finds a creator whose recent cooking lesson has repeated audience questions about substituting leftover vegetables. Offer a single jointly tested recipe and a printable substitution chart; the creator demonstrates it with ingredients they choose, and the app provides an optional plan for the next three dinners. Ask for the relevant audience/placement terms before committing cash. Do not require a positive review or invent a claim about saving money. Track people who actually save and use a plan, not merely recipe views. This is a proposed adaptation, not a documented campaign result.
